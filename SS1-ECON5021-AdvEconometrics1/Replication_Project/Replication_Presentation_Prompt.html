For the second part of the replication project, you will replicate the results identified in your proposal and present your findings to the class. You will give a 5-minute presentation, followed by 3 minutes of questions from me and your classmates.

Replication and Presentation

You will use R to replicate the summary statistics and OLS regression results identified in your proposal.

Your presentation must consist of exactly three slides:

  1. Research Question: Identify the paper, its authors, and the journal in which it was published. Explain the main research question and why it is important. Briefly describe the data used in the analysis.

  2. Regression Model: Present the regression model in equation form. Clearly define the dependent variable, the main explanatory variable, and the control variables. Explain how to interpret the coefficients, particularly the coefficient of interest. Discuss important econometric considerations, such as fixed effects, standard errors, and potential sources of bias.

  3. Replication Results: Present your regression estimates, focusing on the coefficient of interest. Explain its magnitude, statistical significance, and economic significance. Compare your estimates with those reported in the original paper and explain any differences.

Your presentation must last exactly 5 minutes—no more and no less. You should not read from notes or a prepared script. Practice until you can deliver a clear, polished, and concise presentation within the allotted time. A presentation that is too short is just as problematic as one that is too long.

Practice Presentations

You will be assigned two classmates to whom you must give a practice presentation before presenting to the class. Use these sessions to identify unclear explanations, receive feedback, and refine your timing.

The classmates who listen to your practice presentation will be different from those to whom you are assigned to present.

Questions for Other Presenters

You will also be assigned another student’s paper to review. During that student’s presentation, you will be responsible for asking one informed econometric question.

Your question should identify a potential concern with the regression specification or the interpretation of its results. For example, you might ask about omitted variable bias, the choice of control variables, or whether the estimates support a causal interpretation.

The goal is not simply to criticize the paper, but to demonstrate your understanding of econometrics by raising a substantive question that could help assess or strengthen the credibility of the findings. Your question should explain the concern clearly and give the presenter an opportunity to respond.

You will know which paper you have been assigned in advance, but you will not have access to the other student’s presentation before it is delivered.

What to Submit

You will submit the following on Canvas:

  1. Presentation: A PDF containing your three presentation slides.

  2. R Code: A single R script containing the code used to produce your replication results, including the summary statistics and regression estimates. Organize your code so that I can review your work and understand how you obtained your results.

  3. Practice Presentations: Post a comment on this assignment listing the names of the two students whose practice presentations you observed, along with the date and time of each presentation.

You do not need to submit the data, intermediate files, or any other materials. Your R script does not need to be executable on my computer; I simply want to review the code behind your replication.
