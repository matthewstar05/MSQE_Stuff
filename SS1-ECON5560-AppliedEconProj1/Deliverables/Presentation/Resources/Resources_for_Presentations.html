Academic Settings

Here the audience is particularly skeptical of AI-generated slides and the tone is usually very dry, not much color. No emojis. That is considered a distraction.

Economists

Jesse Shapiro's Notes and Lectures page: How to give an applied micro talk: Unauthoritative notes. Real-talk about audiences.

Florian Zettlemeyer's Pointers for Presentations. Titles, transitions, storytelling.

Scientists, more broadly

MIT Communication Lab: https://mitcommlab.mit.edu/broad/commkit/slideshow/

Michael Alley’s assertion-evidence approach at Penn State: structure, visual aids, delivery (informative titles like Zettlemeyer and Duarte)

Other Professional Settings

Pay close attention to the norms for the organization or business. There is a lot of variation, and there may be more openness or tolerance to AI-generated slides, particularly in start ups or tech companies, but there is still a lot of criticism because they generally are not very good.

In professional settings, slides can also serve as an alternative to a written artifact (a document). They can be sent ahead as a pre-read for a meeting or shared after as a take-away. In this case you usually want two versions - a slidedoc that is more complete (more words) and one that is more spare for presenting.  

Nancy Duarte, Slidedocs, has great resources related to this. One thing I took away from her that applies to both slides and slide docs is point is to think of the slide as a set of quadrants and use the layout strategically and with discipline.
