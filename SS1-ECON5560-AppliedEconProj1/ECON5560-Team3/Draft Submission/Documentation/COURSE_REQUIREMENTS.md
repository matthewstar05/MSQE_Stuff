## The memo
- **Length/format:** about five pages, single-spaced (about 2,500 words, excluding references, tables and figures), 11–12 pt, 1-inch margins. One memo per team, with an **attribution list** of who did what.
- **Tone:** a policy brief with scholarly grounding. It should read cleanly for a smart non-specialist.
- **Sections:**
  1. *The problem & the paper* (about ½ page): the real-world question and the headline result, stated plainly.
  2. *What we did* (about 1 page): data, method, what we could and couldn't reproduce, and where it broke down.
  3. *Appraisal for a methods audience* (about 1–1½ pages): do the methods support the claims? Limitations, and how the paper sits in the literature.
  4. *Recommendations for a real-world audience* (about 1 page): plain-language takeaways for a non-technical stakeholder, including what to be cautious about.
  5. *References* (not counted in the page limit).
- **Literature:** at least **5 scholarly sources** beyond the replicated paper, in one consistent style (APA or Chicago author-date), cited for specific claims.
- **AI and citations:** verify that every AI-suggested citation exists and says what we claim. Include a 1–2 sentence **AI-use disclosure**.
- **Grading focus:** clarity of documentation, soundness of the appraisal, and usefulness of the recommendations. Matching the paper's numbers is *not* what's graded.

## The presentation
- About 6–8 minutes plus questions, built around a **two-slide appraisal core**. A few framing slides around it are fine.
- **Arc:** paper & question → what we attempted and found → appraisal → recommendations (land here).
- **Norms (graded):** don't read the slides and don't use written notes; few words per slide, more visual than textual; the whole team engaged with smooth handoffs; handle questions credibly ("we're not sure, but here's our best thinking"); rehearse to time.
