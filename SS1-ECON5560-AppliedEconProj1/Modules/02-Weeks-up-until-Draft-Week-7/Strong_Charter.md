# Strong Charter

# Good Example of a Team Charter


*Specific language.*







**Team name / members:** Team Tideline — Priya, Marcus, Elena, Dan


 


**Project:** Replication of a coastal water-quality recreation study, for a beach-advocacy client








- ** Our goal for this project** We want to produce a solid, honest replication and a one-page recommendation the client could actually use — not just a passing grade. We'd rather document a failed replication clearly than fake a clean result. Target: strong work we're proud to show a real local org.



 



- ** Roles**



 



- **Point of contact (client + instructor):** Priya — she sends and receives all outside emails so we speak with one voice.

- **Note-taker / organizer:** Marcus — keeps the shared doc, logs decisions, tracks who's doing what.

- **Data/analysis lead:** Elena — owns the replication code and dataset, but everyone reviews.

- **Writing/presentation lead:** Dan — owns the deliverable draft and slides, but everyone contributes content.

- Roles are *who's accountable for it getting done*, not who does it alone.



 



- ** How we communicate**



 



- Day-to-day: a group text thread for quick stuff; shared Google Drive folder for all files.

- Response expectation: reply within 24 hours on weekdays (weekends optional).

- Meetings: 45 min every Tuesday after class, video on if remote. Marcus posts an agenda the night before; if you can't make it, you tell the group beforehand and read the notes.



 



- ** How we make decisions** Small stuff: the person who owns that piece decides. Big stuff (scope, what we tell the client, final deliverable): we talk it out and go with majority; if it's 2–2, Priya breaks the tie so we don't stall.



 



- ** If someone doesn't deliver, we will** Step 1: whoever notices checks in directly and privately — assume a good reason first. Step 2: if it happens again or there's no response in 48 hours, raise it with the whole team at the next meeting. Step 3: if it's still unresolved, we flag it to the instructor together, and it will show up honestly in our peer evaluations. No silent resentment, no covering for a no-show without addressing it.



 



- ** How we'll handle disagreement** Raise it early and directly, to the person or the group — not by venting to one teammate about another. Disagreeing about the work is fine and expected; we attack the problem, not each other.



 



- ** Our AI-use agreement**



 



- **How we'll use it:** AI is fine for understanding the paper, debugging code, drafting text, and explaining methods. It is *not* a substitute for us understanding our own analysis.

- **How we'll check it:** anything AI produces that goes into the deliverable — code, numbers, claims — gets checked by a human who understands it. We never paste an AI result we can't explain ourselves. Specifically: if AI writes analysis code, Elena confirms it does what we think before we trust the output.

- **How we'll disclose it:** we keep a short note of where we used AI meaningfully, and include an AI-use line in the final deliverable, per the course norm.



 



- ** We agree to the above** Priya P. · Marcus L. · Elena R. · Dan K.
